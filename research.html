David Vizgan

[CII] as a gas tracer

The 158 μm fine-structure line of singly ionized carbon, [CII], is one of the brightest emission lines in star-forming galaxies and the workhorse line for studying the distant universe. At the Cosmic Dawn Center, I wrote two papers (2022a, 2022b) using hydrodynamical simulations to calibrate [CII] as a tracer of molecular and atomic gas in galaxies at z = 6. As a PhD student, I now study [CII] under the cosmic microscope, using ALMA observations of gravitationally lensed galaxies to contextualize it within the high-redshift interstellar medium.

Galaxy-galaxy strong lensing

Current and incoming surveys from Rubin/LSST, Roman, and Euclid will uncover hundreds of thousands of galaxy-galaxy lenses. My PhD work focuses on developing and improving the methods, tools, and diagnostics that will enable scientists to study these enormous datasets.

Ring galaxies

Collisional ring galaxies — rare systems whose star-forming rings are produced by a smaller galaxy plunging through the disk of a larger one — can closely resemble Einstein rings. I discovered the most distant ring galaxy known to date, at z = 4.0148, and discussed how such objects act as contaminants in searches for gravitational lenses.
